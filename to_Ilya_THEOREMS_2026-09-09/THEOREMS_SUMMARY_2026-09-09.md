## Setting

Let λ be a minuscule weight of a simply-laced simple root system with
Weyl group W and Coxeter number h, and let Wλ be its weights. The
weights form a distributive lattice L (covers w → w − α_i where the
label ⟨w, α_i^∨⟩ = 1); its join-irreducibles form the minuscule poset
P = P_λ, each element coloured by the simple root of its unique cover.
Rowmotion R on the order ideals of P, R(I) = the ideal generated by the
minimal elements of P ∖ I, is carried to Wλ by I ↦ join(I). By Rush–Shi
(2013), R is conjugate in Sym(Wλ) to any Coxeter element c acting on Wλ;
hence R has order h and a Coxeter element's cycle type. A Coxeter
element is an isometry of Wλ; R in general is not. We ask when R, or a
power of R, is itself an element of W, and how far it is otherwise.

Three facts from the literature are used. (i) The multiplicity of colour
i in P equals the coefficient c_i of α_i in λ − w₀λ = λ + λ*. (ii) P is
graded, with h − 1 ranks, and the R-orbit of the empty ideal is the
sequence of rank truncations P_{<j}. (iii) The minuscule weights are
ω_k of A_n; ω₁, ω_{n−1}, ω_n of D_n; ω₁, ω₆ of E₆; ω₇ of E₇ (and ω_n of
B_n, ω₁ of C_n, not treated here). Normalize ⟨α, α⟩ = 2, so that
⟨w, α_i⟩ is the i-th label of w.

**Notation.** For λ = ω_k write c_k for the multiplicity of colour k in
P; s_j = |{p ∈ P : rank p = j}| for 0 ≤ j ≤ r − 1 = h − 2; and the
*extended rank sequence* σ = (s₀, …, s_{r−1}, c_k), of length h.

## Lemma (two inner products)

(a) R(λ) = w₀λ, and the bottom w₀λ has exactly one cover, p₀ = w₀λ +
α_{k*} where k* is the node dual to k; p₀ is the unique minimal element
of P and R(w₀λ) = p₀. Consequently ⟨λ, w₀λ⟩ = ⟨λ,λ⟩ − c_k while
⟨w₀λ, p₀⟩ = ⟨λ,λ⟩ − 1.

(b) Let w(j) be the weight of the rank truncation P_{<j}, so that
w(0) = w₀λ, w(h−1) = λ, and R(w(j)) = w(j+1) with indices mod h. Then for
every j, ⟨w(j), w(j+1)⟩ = ⟨λ,λ⟩ − σ_j.

*Proof.* (a) The bottom −λ* has a single nonzero label, −1 at k*, so a
single cover; the ideal ∅ maps to the ideal generated by min P = {p₀}.
⟨λ, λ − w₀λ⟩ = Σ c_i ⟨ω_k, α_i⟩ = c_k. (b) w(j+1) − w(j) is the sum of
the simple roots α_{c(p)} over the rank-j elements p; each is added at a
label −1 of the current weight, so ⟨w(j), α_{c(p)}⟩ = −1 and the inner
product drops by s_j; the wrap-around step λ → w₀λ drops by c_k by (a).
All weights have the same norm. ∎

## Theorem 1 (rowmotion is linear only on chains)

R ∈ W if and only if P is a chain, i.e. λ ∈ {ω₁, ω_n} of type A_n. In
that case R is the Coxeter element s₁ s₂ ⋯ s_n (up to the dual), the
cyclic shift of e₁, …, e_{n+1}.

*Proof.* If R ∈ W it preserves ⟨·,·⟩, so by the Lemma (a) applied to the
pair (λ, w₀λ) ↦ (w₀λ, p₀): ⟨λ,λ⟩ − c_k = ⟨λ,λ⟩ − 1, i.e. c_k = 1. From
the classification: c_k = min(k, n+1−k) for A_n ω_k; 2 for D_n ω₁;
⌊n/2⌋ for D_n ω_n (n ≥ 4); 2 for E₆ ω₁; 3 for E₇ ω₇. So c_k = 1 only for
A_n with k = 1 or n, whose poset is the chain e₁ > … > e_{n+1}. There the
ideals are the initial segments and R is the cyclic shift, a permutation
matrix in W(A_n) = S_{n+1}. ∎

## Theorem 2 (the half-turn is linear only on vector representations)

Let h be even and m = h/2. Then R^m ∈ W if and only if P is a chain or
λ is the vector representation of D_n, n ≥ 3 (including D₃ ω₁ = A₃ ω₂
and, for D₄, the triality images ω₃, ω₄ of ω₁). In the vector case,
with weights ±e_i,

  R^{n−1}(e_i) = −e_{n−i} (1 ≤ i ≤ n−1),  R^{n−1}(e_n) = (−1)^{n−1} e_n,

a signed permutation with an even number of sign changes, hence in
W(D_n).

*Proof.* (Obstruction.) If R^m ∈ W then R^m is an isometry with
R^m(w(j)) = w(j+m), so by the Lemma (b), σ_{j+m} = σ_j for all j: the
extended rank sequence is m-periodic. Since σ₀ = s₀ = 1 (p₀ is the only
minimal element), σ_m = 1 is necessary. Case by case: A_n ω_k with
1 < k < n (n odd, m = (n+1)/2): P is the k × (n+1−k) rectangle and
s_m = min(k, n+1−k) ≥ 2. D_n ω_n, n ≥ 5 (m = n−1): P is the shifted
staircase {(i,j) : 1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ n−1} with rank i + j − 2, and
s_{n−1} = ⌊(n+1)/2⌋ − 1 ≥ 2. E₆ ω₁ (m = 6): ranks 1,1,1,2,2,2,2,2,1,1,1,
s₆ = 2. E₇ ω₇ (m = 9): ranks 1,1,1,1,2,2,2,2,3,2,2,2,2,1,1,1,1, s₉ = 2.
D_n ω₁ (m = n−1): P is a chain of n−2 above the antichain {e_n, −e_n}
above a chain of n−2, with c₁ = 2, so σ = (1^{n−2}, 2, 1^{n−2}, 2) is
(n−1)-periodic; the triality images of ω₁ at n = 4 and the chains pass
likewise. (Sufficiency.) On D_n ω₁ the ideals are ∅, the n−2 prefixes of
the lower chain, the three ideals adding e_n, −e_n, or both, and the
n−2 extensions along the upper chain; rowmotion cycles ∅ → … → P in
2n−2 steps and swaps the two one-sided ideals. On weights:
−e₁ → −e₂ → ⋯ → −e_{n−1} → e_{n−1} → e_{n−2} → ⋯ → e₁ → −e₁, and
e_n ↔ −e_n. Hence R^{n−1}(±e_i) = ∓e_{n−i} for i ≤ n−1 and R^{n−1}(e_n)
= (−1)^{n−1}e_n. This is the coordinate permutation i ↔ n−i with sign
changes on e₁, …, e_{n−1} and, for n even, on e_n: n changes for n even,
n−1 for n odd, even in both cases, so R^{n−1} ∈ W(D_n). ∎

**Corollary.** For any j, R^j ∈ W implies that σ is j-periodic. Hence
{j : R^j ∈ W} is contained in the set of periods of σ. For E₇, σ has no
period smaller than 18, so no power of the clock except the identity is
linear — the statement C_W(Ψ) ∩ ⟨Ψ⟩ = 1 of SM-054, now by hand.

## Theorem 3 (the kept fraction, two families)

Let κ(R) be the fraction of unordered pairs {w, w′} ⊂ Wλ with
⟨Rw, Rw′⟩ = ⟨w, w′⟩. Then

  (a) κ(D_n ω₁) = 1 − 2(n−1) / (n(2n−1)),  n ≥ 3;
  (b) κ(A_n ω₂) = 1 − (3n² − 9n + 4) / C(C(n+1,2), 2),  n ≥ 3.

*Proof.* (a) Pair types are 0 and −1 (the n antipodal pairs). From the
cycle in Theorem 2, {e_n, −e_n} is the only antipodal pair R preserves;
the other n−1 become non-antipodal, and since R permutes the set of
pairs, exactly n−1 non-antipodal pairs become antipodal. So 2(n−1) of
the n(2n−1) pairs change type.

(b) Weights are the 2-subsets {a < b} of [n+1] (e_a + e_b); the type of
a pair of subsets is |S ∩ T| ∈ {0, 1}. The ideals of the 2 × (n−1)
rectangle are pairs (x₁ ≥ x₂) of row lengths, corresponding to the
subset {n − x₁, n + 1 − x₂}; computing rowmotion on the rectangle gives

  R{a, b} = {a−1, b−1}  (a ≥ 2, b ≥ a+2),   R{a, a+1} = {a−1, n+1}  (a ≥ 2),
  R{1, b} = {b−2, b−1}  (b ≥ 3),            R{1, 2} = {n, n+1}.

Let ρ be the rotation i ↦ i−1 (mod n+1), a Coxeter element and an
isometry. Then R = ρ ∘ φ where φ fixes every subset outside
T = {{a, a+1} : 2 ≤ a ≤ n} ∪ {{1, b} : 2 ≤ b ≤ n+1} and permutes T by
{a, a+1} ↦ {1, a}, {1, b} ↦ {b−1, b} (b ≥ 3), {1, 2} ↦ {1, n+1}. So
κ(R) = κ(φ), and only pairs meeting T can change type. For S ∈ T and a
generic G ∉ T: the pair changes type iff a+1 ∈ G (S = {a, a+1}), iff
b−1 ∈ G (S = {1, b}, b ≥ 3), or iff exactly one of 2, n+1 lies in G
(S = {1, 2}); counting generic subsets gives 2(n−2)² + 2(n−3). For pairs
inside T: of the (n−2) + 2(n−1) + C(n, 2) intersecting pairs, exactly
4n − 5 remain intersecting after φ (all adjacent {a,a+1},{a+1,a+2}; all
({a,a+1}, {1,a}) and ({a,a+1}, {1,a+1}); among the {1,b}'s only the n−2
pairs with |b − b′| = 1 and the pair ({1,2}, {1,n+1})), so
(n−1)(n−2) pairs change. In total 3n² − 9n + 4 of the C(C(n+1,2), 2)
pairs change type. ∎

*Checked* for n = 3..9 in exact arithmetic (e.g. n = 8: 253/315 and
53/60; n = 9: 412/495 and 137/153), and the explicit R on 2-subsets
against the machine's rowmotion for each n.

## Observations, not claimed

- On A_n ω₃ the number of type-changing pairs D₃(n) was registered as a
  degree-4 polynomial and confirmed to n = 10: 74, 256, 678, 1480, 2832,
  4934, with 12·D₃(n) = 15n⁴ − 110n³ + 165n² + 314n − 432. With
  D₂(n) = 3n² − 9n + 4 this suggests degree 2k − 2 for ω_k; a proof would
  follow Theorem 3(b) on the k × (n+1−k) rectangle.
- On A_n ω₂ the rotation ρ and R agree on the C(n,2) − (n−1) generic
  subsets, so the best Coxeter agreement a(R) is at least that, with
  equality for n = 5..8.
- The E₆ and E₇ clocks fail every one of the linearity conditions above
  at the first place they can: c_k = 2 and 3; σ not periodic at all. In
  the family, the 56-board's clock is as far from linear as rowmotion
  on a minuscule poset gets.

## What is not claimed

Nothing for B_n, C_n, or for non-minuscule posets; no formula for κ on
the spinors, E₆, E₇, or A_n ω_k with k ≥ 3; the order and orbit
structure of R (Rush–Shi) are cited, not derived.
