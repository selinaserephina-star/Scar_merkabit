## Step 1: Does the model contain an object that can be mathematically called a "vortex"?

| Candidate object | Definition requires | Present in the model? |
|---|---|---|
| Vector field | A vector assigned to each point of some space, v(x) | **No.** φ₂, Φ₃, Φ₃′ are fixed vectors (VEVs) — constants, not functions of any coordinate. Nothing in the model varies over a space. |
| Circulation | ∮v·dl along a closed loop | **No.** No loop is defined; no field to integrate along one. |
| Curl / rotor | ∇×v, a spatial derivative of a vector field | **No**, in the strict sense — there is no space to differentiate over. **Partial overlap:** the cross product χ×ξ (used in the Φ₃⊗Φ₃′ operator) is built from the same Levi-Civita antisymmetric contraction ε_ijk that appears in curl's definition. But curl requires a *derivative* of a field; χ and ξ are constants, and the curl of a constant field is identically zero. χ×ξ is a bilinear antisymmetric form between two fixed vectors — same algebraic ingredient as curl, not the same operation. |
| Twist (закрутка) | Varies by convention | **One genuine hit, of a different kind:** ρ₃′(g) = sign(g)·ρ₃(g) is legitimately called a "twist" in representation theory (tensoring by a character). This is a **discrete ±1 sign twist**, not a continuous rotation/circulation — unrelated to curl or vortex flow. |
