## 1. What was wrong

The prior construction assigned **both** Φ₃ and Φ₃′ Froggatt–Nielsen charge 2 (i.e. both ~ ε² from the same flaton η), reasoning that they play parallel roles as the two triplets inside χ₈|S₄ = 2⊕3⊕3′. A scan over discrete axis choices, ε²-vs-ε³ power reassignment, and a free continuous angle for φ₂ (36+ independent fits) all robustly converged on |c₃′| ≈ 31–35 — never O(1).

## 2. Why ε³ (the tested "fix") makes it worse, not better

The physically required |Φ₃′| VEV, from the exact mass fit, is a fixed number: **0.1131 Λ** (in units where Λ=1). Dividing this fixed requirement by different candidate ε-powers:

```text
0.1131 / eps    = 1.88   <- O(1)
0.1131 / eps^2  = 31.4   <- what the original construction used
0.1131 / eps^3  = 523    <- worse
```

Trying ε³ (a natural-seeming next guess) moves further from O(1), not closer, because the *required magnitude itself* doesn't change — only how it's split between an ε-power and a coefficient. The 31.4 was never really "we need one more power of ε"; it was "Φ₃′'s true scale is close to ε, not ε²."

## 3. The fix: Φ₃′ at FN-charge 1, same order as φ₂

Reassigning charges — φ₂: charge 1, Φ₃: charge 2 (unchanged), **Φ₃′: charge 1** (not 2) — gives Φ₃′ ~ ε·Λ, the same suppression order as φ₂ itself. Refitting:

```text
<phi2>  = eps*(0,1)
<Phi3>  = eps^2 * c3  * (axis)
<Phi3'> = eps^1 * c3' * (axis, different from Phi3's axis)

Best point: y2=0.05201  c3=0.5365  c3'=1.8842   (cost ~1e-29, exact)
```

**Robustness check** (6 independent axis-pair combinations, φ₂ angle left fully free, not fixed to (1,0)/(0,1)):

| | y₂ | c₃ | c₃′ |
|---|---|---|---|
| range | 0.046 – 0.049 | 0.50 – 1.05 | 2.10 – 2.39 |
| mean | ~0.048 | 0.93 | 2.15 |

All three coefficients land cleanly in O(1) territory across every combination tried, with no outliers. This is not a single lucky point — it's the generic behavior of this charge assignment.

## 6. One new honest question this raises

Φ₃ and Φ₃′ now carry **different** FN charges (2 and 1 respectively) despite
both originating from the same parent representation χ₈|S₄ = 2⊕3⊕3′. This
needs its own justification — it is a much more ordinary model-building
question than "why is this coefficient 31," but it is not free. A natural
candidate: since 3′ = 3 ⊗ sign, an FN charge correlated with a Z₂ grading
tied to the sign representation (e.g. Φ₃′ coupling to a Z₂-odd companion
flaton, or to η itself with an extra sign-representation insertion) would
produce exactly this charge split from a single underlying discrete choice,
rather than two independently-chosen integers. This is not yet constructed
or verified — it is the next concrete item, not a solved problem.
