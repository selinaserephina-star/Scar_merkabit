## What was done

Beyond the three non-specialist searches §7.4 already discloses, this pass
used zbMATH Open (free access since 2021, zbmath.org) to follow the full
citation trail of the paper the entire spine rests on: **Rush, D. B.; Shi,
X., "On orbits of order ideals of minuscule posets," J. Algebr. Comb. 37,
No. 3, 545–569 (2013), Zbl 1283.06007** — 2 reviews, 30 citing documents,
all 30 read. This is a different search method than the general-web passes
already disclosed: it surfaces everything that cites the specific paper
the identification depends on, not just keyword matches.

## Result: still not found, with two related-but-distinct results worth citing

None of the 30 citing documents states or proves that rowmotion on a
minuscule poset is itself a Weyl group element (an isometry) if and only
if the poset is a chain, or that its half-turn is a Weyl element if and
only if the poset is the vector representation of D_n, or the per-pair
parity rule of §7.2. The 30 span cyclic sieving and periodicity extensions
(Mandel–Pechenik on the two exceptional flag varieties; Patrias–Pechenik
×2), further homomesy and CDE results (Hopkins ×3; Haddadan; Dilks–
Pechenik–Striker), classification and structural work (Strayer; Bolognini–
Sentinelli), other poset families (trapezoid, m-Tamari, fences — the last
of which notes rowmotion's order is not even known in general there),
promotion/birational variants (Hopkins–Joseph; Musiker–Roby; Dilks–
Striker–Vorland; Bernstein–Striker–Vorland; Chiang et al.), and root-poset
antichain work in other directions (Dong–Weng; Dong–Wang; Panyushev 2015)
— none asks whether rowmotion itself is linear.

Two results, found this way, are close enough in language that a
specialist reader will likely raise them, and are worth citing in §7.4
directly rather than waiting to be asked:

**Marczinzik, R.; Thomas, H.; Yıldırım, E., "On the interaction of the
Coxeter transformation and the rowmotion bijection," J. Comb. Algebra 8
(2024), 359–374, doi:10.4171/JCA/101.** Studies rowmotion (as a
permutation matrix) together with a "Coxeter matrix," proving
(ρ⁻¹C)² = id (a conjecture of Sam Hopkins). Their C is homological —
built from the Cartan matrix of the incidence algebra's indecomposable
projectives (C = M⁻¹Mᵀ) — not the root system's inner product. Same two
words, "rowmotion" and "Coxeter," a different object entirely; worth one
sentence in §7.4 distinguishing them, since Hugh Thomas (already in the
draft's bibliography via "Rowmotion in slow motion") and Yıldırım are
both active in exactly this adjacent space.

**Panyushev, D. I., "Weight posets associated with gradings of simple Lie
algebras, Weyl groups, and arrangements of hyperplanes," J. Algebr.
Comb., arXiv:1412.0987** — the direct sequel to his 2015 paper that is
one of the 30 citations. Gives "a bijection between the lower ideals in
Δ(1) and certain elements of the Weyl group" — but this is an indexing
bijection (which Weyl-group element labels which ideal), not a claim
that rowmotion itself, as a permutation of the weights, coincides with
one. Adjacent enough to flag; not a match.
