## 1. Why this object

The bitangent-bridge theorem (T-BR) showed the two programs meet at E₇:
inside Sp₆(2) = W(E₇)/{±1}, W(E₆) is the stabilizer of one bitangent of the
Klein quartic and PSL(2,7) permutes all 28, intersecting in S₃ = N(⟨z₃⟩).
Neither framework's native object carries both symmetries. **The E₇ minuscule
crystal — 56 vertices — is the smallest object that does**: its weights are
the 28 bitangent pairs doubled (56 = 2 × 28, classical), our crystal dynamics
already runs on it (the E₇ thread), and Ilya's 3A class maps 2:1 onto its
pair-quotient (T-BR, computed). A machine built on the 56 is the natural
*joint* architecture: her gates, his grading — the thing to build **with**
Ilya, not for him.

## 3. The design

**State space.** The 56 crystal vertices, addressed as (bitangent, sign):
28 frames × 1 bit, via the antipodal quotient ι. In bridge language: a
*frame register* (which bitangent = which "point of view", the object
PSL(2,7) moves) and a *chirality bit* (the ± the E₇ weight doubling adds).

**Gate inventory (three families).**
- *Clock gates*: Ψ, Ψ⁶, Ψ⁹ — even, order 18/3/2. The CSP census on the
  [18,18,18,2] orbits is the tamper-evident invariant, as on the 27.
- *Diagram gate*: pr — the odd involution. E₇'s gift: **native parity access**
  (the 27-machine cannot make an odd move; the 56-machine can). Universality
  ⟨pr,Ψ⁶⟩ = S₅₆ is already sealed.
- *Geometric gate*: ι — the antipode / bitangent-pair flip. NOT generated by
  the clock (Ψ⁹-pairing ≠ ι-pairing): a genuinely independent instruction.

**Type system (his grading).** PSL(2,7) ⊂ Sp₆(2) acts on the 28 frames
transitively with stabilizer S₃. The typed subgroup of the 56-machine is the
lift of that action; "Scar-typed" circuits are those commuting with the
grading, and the **Scar-magic monotone μ_SC(g) = d_H(g, Scar-typed)/56** is
the E₇ analogue of CN-011's μ — measuring how much a program mixes Ilya's
frames, exactly as μ measured mixing of Selina's registers. Two-axis resource
theory: (μ_typed, μ_SC) — a circuit can be cheap in one grammar and expensive
in the other.

**Error syndrome.** The affine E₇ Dynkin diagram has **8 nodes** — and 8 is
Ilya's other number: |P¹(𝔽₇)|, the even theta characteristic's world (T-BR:
his group fixes exactly one even form, stabilizer 8! = O₆⁺(2) ≅ S₈). Whether
the 8-channel E₇ syndrome relates to his 8-point action is DQ-3 below — the
E₆ Fano-syndrome precedent (`verify_fano_syndrome.py`: NOT organized, but
exactly one bit away, a chiral Fano pair) tells us to expect a sharp
compatible-vs-forced answer, and to compute it, not assume it.

## 4. Design questions (each one afternoon-sized, compute-first)

- **DQ-1 (the lift).** W(E₇) = ℤ₂ × Sp₆(2) (direct product), so PSL(2,7)
  lifts to W(E₇) acting on the 56 weights. Orbits: one 56-orbit or two
  28-orbits? (Equivalently: does the lifted action mix the ± sheets?) This
  decides whether the frame register and chirality bit are independent under
  his group.
- **DQ-2 (grading vs gates).** Compute μ_SC(Ψ), μ_SC(pr), μ_SC(ι) exactly;
  find which machine gates are Scar-typed. Conjecture-free zone: we have no
  prediction.
- **DQ-3 (the 8-channel syndrome).** Build the affine-E₇ syndrome (8
  channels), compute its motion group (expect the diagram's tiny symmetry),
  and run the analogue of the Fano check against P¹(𝔽₇) structures
  (PGL(2,7)-sets on 8 labels). The E₆ answer was "one bit short"; the E₇
  answer is open.
- **DQ-4 (two pairings).** The machine carries two canonical 28-pairings
  (ι and Ψ⁹). Their joint group ⟨ι, Ψ⁹⟩, the overlap structure (4 shared
  pairs — why 4? the [2]-orbit?), and whether ιΨι = Ψ⁻¹ holds on the 56.
- **DQ-5 (S₃ everywhere).** Per-frame stabilizer S₃ (his) vs the E₆
  sublattice: fixing a frame should recover a 27-flavoured world inside the
  56 (bitangent stabilizer = W(E₆) story). Make "descend from the 56-machine
  to the 27-machine by fixing a frame" a literal operation.
